# Seven family room recovery

The private room coordinator recovers selected clients from an admitted physical
catalog without changing their native history, file family or provider contract.
It supports original and generation clients, independent local and complete
delivery clients, restored delivery graphs, and local snapshot and flat inputs.
All selected clients cross one existing handoff barrier or none is returned.

## Source custody and current authority

Recovery begins by transferring a genuine catalog's database, archive and lease.
A selection, saved completion flag or readiness-shaped object cannot supply that
ownership. Cold recovery independently reads the retained catalog and intent.

The recovery job and each client have separate authority and cancellation
policies. Ending the finite job must not disable an independently authorized
returned editor. Conversely, the job's authority cannot grant a client permission
to edit. Every cold open selects current factories and permissions again.

Complete delivery requires an explicit read-only staging factory as well as its
current source factory. A catalog graph uses its complete `lineage-v2` file,
including the latest current, pending and live state; its earlier input file is
provenance, not a substitute recovery source. No source is flattened into another
family to make admission succeed.

## Whole selection validation

Before child recovery, the coordinator snapshots ordinary policy values and
checks all selected identities, component names and current factory contracts.
It rejects aliased destinations and duplicate selected writers or comment actors.
Historical copies remain retained when they are not selected; they are never
discarded or counted as independently recovered writers.

Own data fields and dense arrays prevent getters, sparse entries or replaced
array methods from choosing a different destination during preparation. Runtime
driver, origin and bootstrap markers are checked on the actual supplied objects
before copying. Removing such a marker through a plain copy must not turn an
internal factory into a fresh host policy. Functions and explicitly opaque host
handles retain their identities; executable policy is never persisted.

Database names retain their existing opaque spelling, including spaces and
Unicode. Catalog and participant identifiers retain their existing restricted
identifier grammar. Neither is normalized into another namespace.

## Durable intent and interruption

A separate intent records the immutable source pin, exact destination roles and
ordered `reserved`, `started` and `complete` states. Its own schema and version do
not extend an older catalog, journal or native protocol. Creation is absent-only.
Each transition compares the actual stored predecessor before committing.

An interrupted acknowledgement can follow a successful commit. Retry reads the
exact previous or pending successor record instead of repeating a write blindly.
Source files and actual child components are re-admitted; a completion marker is
not evidence that their bytes still exist. Missing completed stores refuse
recovery rather than creating empty replacements. Occupied or foreign stores are
not overwritten, migrated or deleted.

The intent store retains actual connections and outstanding requests after
failed or blocked opens. Cleanup can be retried against the same handles. Closing
a blocked request refuses promptly while retaining its eventual cleanup; a late
upgrade cannot publish an empty placeholder.

Progress is a detached, deeply immutable diagnostic. Its namespace arrays cannot
retarget the coordinator's private source or destination pin. An `operational`
phase means the selected client components were prepared, not that historic
copies, unbound comment streams or the server have been reconciled.

## Atomic handoff and provider lifetime

The seven-family router composes the existing original/generation and five-family
reservations through the common kernel. Every genuine owner is busy before host
callbacks begin. Actual native history, retained packets, source epochs and whole
upload custody remain held through the last checks.

Temporary native readers are disposed before final client validation. The job's
last cancellation check reads private observed state, not caller properties.
Only private flag writes follow the final native/storage barrier. Parent
retirement does not dispose returned sources or their source-owned providers.

Legacy local clients keep an `original` provider. Snapshot and flat-local clients
keep an `input` provider. Those literal files are provenance, not backups of later
edits. Journal-backed delivery and graph clients use their complete operation
exporters; the ordinary source export deliberately refuses to omit current or
pending work. Whole uploads, receipts and retained original pixels keep their
existing contracts.

A late handoff refusal can occur after coordinator connections or leases have
been released. No client is returned, and those release flags are not reset to
simulate a warm retry. Close the retained attempt and cold-resume from the exact
intent, catalog and child stores under fresh current policy. Cleanup remains
available after authority or cancellation ends.
